WireForge privacy statement
===========================

WireForge is a program that runs on your own PC. It has no account, no
telemetry, no advertising and no automatic update check. Its author does not
receive any data from it.

What WireForge stores, and where
--------------------------------
Everything is kept in one folder on your PC, %LOCALAPPDATA%\WireForge:

  settings.json    your settings
  usage.json       how many bytes were used per day, adapter and app
  devices.json     devices seen on your local network and the names you gave them
  speedtest.json   the results of speed tests you ran

You can open the folder from Settings and delete any of these files, or the
whole folder, at any time. Saved Wi-Fi passwords are read from Windows when
you open the "Saved networks" page; WireForge does not store them. They are
written to a file only if you choose Export and tick "Include passwords".

When WireForge contacts the internet
------------------------------------
Only when you click:

  Speed test       Runs against Measurement Lab (M-Lab), an open research
                   project. M-Lab's servers see your public IP address.
                   M-Lab keeps the results of every test, including your IP
                   address, the date and time and the measured speeds, and
                   publishes them as open data, indefinitely. WireForge shows
                   you this and asks for your agreement before the first
                   test. No other information about you or your PC is sent.
                   M-Lab privacy policy: https://www.measurementlab.net/privacy/
                   M-Lab acceptable use policy: https://www.measurementlab.net/aup/

  Public IP        "Show" next to "Public IP address" asks api.ipify.org,
                   which answers with the address the request came from.

  Tools            Ping, traceroute, DNS lookup, whois and port check send
                   their probes to the host or server you enter or pick.
                   Choosing a public DNS resolver sends your question to that
                   company.

On your local network
---------------------
The device scan asks each address of the network your PC is directly
attached to who is there (ARP, ping and name queries such as mDNS, NetBIOS
and SSDP). It only ever runs on private address ranges and never looks
outside your local network. It runs when you click Scan, or every ten
minutes if you turn that on.

Host-name lookups for remote addresses (reverse DNS, sent to your own DNS
server) are off unless you turn them on.
